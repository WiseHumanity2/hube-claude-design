# Hu.Be — Visual and Branding Guidelines

**For:** External designers working on Hu.Be materials (decks, one-pagers, web assets)
**Scope:** Hu.Be product brand only
**Audiences:** Prospects and investors (one unified visual identity for both)

---

## Brand Emotional Direction

Everything in this guide serves one feeling: **grounded and calm, from which place people can grow and achieve incredible targets.**

This means the brand should feel like solid ground, not like a launch pad. Not energising, not buzzing, not cheerful. Steady. Present. The kind of visual environment where someone exhales and thinks clearly. The ambition is real, but it comes from depth, not from hype.

Hu.Be is not a wellness app. It is not an analytics dashboard. It is not a motivational tool. It sits inside the daily flow of work and helps people practise better ways of working together, continuously. The visual identity should reflect that: embedded, unhurried, substantial.

## Colour Palette

### The five colours

| Colour | Hex | Name | Role |
|--------|-----|------|------|
| ██ | #0f233d | Dark Navy | Primary. The anchor of the brand. |
| ██ | #005588 | Vibrant Blue | Secondary. Supporting depth and structure. |
| ██ | #fc8747 | Orange | Accent. Warmth, moments of insight, CTAs. |
| ██ | #fed23d | Yellow | Accent. Sparingly. Highlighting, data points. |
| ██ | #ffffff | White | Background, text on dark, breathing space. |

### How to use colour

**Dark Navy (#0f233d) is the dominant colour.** It should be the most present colour on any slide or page. Use it for backgrounds, large text areas, and structural elements. It creates the grounded, sheltering quality the brand needs. It is not coldness or corporate authority. It is depth. Think: the colour of a room where it is safe to be honest.

**Vibrant Blue (#005588) supports the navy.** Use it for secondary text, borders, dividers, and supporting elements. It adds variation without breaking the tonal range. It should never compete with the navy for dominance.

**Orange (#fc8747) is reserved and intentional.** Use it only for: call-to-action buttons, financial figures and key metrics, and moments where the design needs to draw attention to a single point. Orange is the warmth in the room. It should feel like light arriving, not like a siren. If orange appears more than two or three times on a single slide, it is being overused.

**Yellow (#fed23d) is the rarest colour.** Use it for small data highlights, chart accents, or a single emphasis point. Never for backgrounds. Never for large text. It should feel like a brief moment of brightness, not a sustained presence.

**White (#ffffff) provides breathing space.** Use it for text on dark backgrounds and for open areas that give the eye somewhere to rest. White space is not emptiness. It is part of the grounded feeling.

### Colour ratios (approximate guide)

On a typical slide:
- Dark Navy: 50-60%
- Vibrant Blue: 15-20%
- White: 15-20%
- Orange: 5-8%
- Yellow: 0-3%

### What to avoid with colour

- Do not use orange for backgrounds or large filled areas. It overwhelms the grounded tone.
- Do not use light or pastel tints of the palette. No light blue, no peach, no cream. The palette is five colours, not a gradient spectrum.
- Do not introduce greys. Use navy at reduced opacity if a softer tone is needed.
- Do not use yellow and orange next to each other. They merge and lose distinction.
- Do not default to a white background with coloured accents. That is the standard SaaS layout and makes Hu.Be look like every other platform in the category. Lead with navy.

## Typography

### Font family

**Nunito** is the sole typeface. Use the full weight range to create hierarchy and contrast.

| Weight | Use |
|--------|-----|
| Nunito Black (900) | Hero headlines, section titles, the single most important line on a slide. |
| Nunito Bold (700) | Subheadings, emphasis within body text, key phrases in callouts. |
| Nunito SemiBold (600) | Labels, captions, navigation, metadata. |
| Nunito Regular (400) | Body text, descriptions, long-form content. |
| Nunito Light (300) | Use sparingly. Pull quotes, secondary annotations, large-format display text where lightness adds elegance. |

### Typography rules

**Create hierarchy through weight, not through font variety.** The difference between Nunito Black and Nunito Regular is significant. Use that range fully. Do not introduce a second typeface.

**Headlines should be large and confident.** Minimum 28pt in a deck context. They are the first thing the eye hits. They should feel like someone speaking clearly, not like a label.

**Body text should be comfortable to read.** 14-16pt in a deck, with generous line spacing (1.4-1.5x). Do not compress text to fit more on a slide. If the text doesn't fit, there is too much text.

**Do not use all-caps for extended text.** All-caps can work for very short labels (2-3 words) but never for headlines or sentences. It reads as shouting, which contradicts the grounded tone.

**Do not use italic for emphasis.** Use a heavier weight instead. Italic in Nunito is subtle enough to be missed and creates visual noise in body text.

### What to avoid with typography

- Do not introduce a second font "for contrast." Nunito's weight range provides all the contrast needed.
- Do not use decorative or script fonts anywhere, including for quotes or callouts.
- Do not set body text below 12pt in any context.
- Do not centre-align body text. Left-align all body copy. Centre alignment is acceptable only for standalone headlines or single-line callouts.

## Imagery Direction

### The principle

Every image should answer the question: **"What does it actually feel like to be on this team?"** If an image does not convey a feeling about the human experience of working together, it does not belong in Hu.Be materials.

### Photography guidelines

**Use real photography wherever possible.** Wise Humanity has genuine workshop photos and team images. These should be the first choice, always. They are authentic, distinctive, and impossible for any competitor to replicate.

**When stock photography is unavoidable, apply these filters:**

- People should look like they are mid-conversation, not mid-pose. No one should be looking at the camera.
- The setting should feel like a real workplace, not a showroom. Slight imperfection is better than polish. Coffee cups, notebooks, a slightly messy table — these are signs of life.
- Diverse representation should be present but not performative. If the diversity of the image is the first thing you notice, it was cast, not captured.
- No glass-walled offices. No standing around a whiteboard with sticky notes. No hands-in-a-huddle team-spirit shots. No one high-fiving. No one looking at a laptop and smiling. These are the most overused images in HR tech and make the brand instantly generic.
- No outdoor lifestyle photos (people walking on a beach, hiking, laughing in a park) unless they have a direct connection to the content. Aspirational filler erodes trust.

**Image tone should match the colour palette.** Favour images with warm, muted tones. Avoid images that are overly bright, saturated, or studio-lit. If an image needs colour correction to sit comfortably alongside navy and orange, correct it. If it resists correction, choose a different image.

### The Sherpa mascot

The Sherpa is Hu.Be's branded character. It is distinctive, ownable, and no competitor has anything like it. Use it with care.

**When to use the Sherpa:**

- As a visual anchor on key slides: the opening, the closing, transition points between major sections.
- When illustrating the co-pilot concept: the Sherpa alongside a team or alongside a workflow, present but not dominant.
- In UI mockups and product demonstrations where the Sherpa represents the platform's voice.
- To add warmth and personality to an otherwise data-heavy or structural slide.

**When not to use the Sherpa:**

- Do not place the Sherpa on every slide. Overuse reduces it from a distinctive character to a decorative logo. Aim for no more than 30-40% of slides.
- Do not use the Sherpa as a replacement for substantive content. It should complement a point, not fill a gap.
- Do not show the Sherpa in isolation on an otherwise empty slide (except potentially a closing/thank-you slide).

**How to use the Sherpa:**

- The Sherpa should always feel like it is alongside people, not in front of them. It walks with the team, not ahead of it. It sits in the room, not at the podium.
- Scale appropriately. On content-heavy slides, the Sherpa should be smaller — a presence in the margin, not competing with the information. On character-driven slides (opening, philosophy), it can be larger.
- **Do not composite the Sherpa into stock meeting-room photos.** This was identified as a specific weakness on the current website. It places a distinctive character inside a generic visual and undermines both. If the Sherpa appears alongside people, use illustration-style environments or real Wise Humanity photography, not stock images.
- Maintain consistent illustration quality. If new Sherpa images are generated, they should match the established style: warm colours (navy and orange clothing), friendly but not cartoonish, carrying the walking staff.

### Illustrations and diagrams

- Use the brand palette strictly. Diagrams, charts, and illustrations should use only the five colours.
- Favour clean, structural diagrams over decorative illustrations. Flowcharts, layered models, and comparison frameworks are more useful and more credible than conceptual artwork.
- Avoid generic SaaS iconography (lightbulbs for ideas, gears for process, rockets for growth, targets for goals). If an icon is needed, keep it simple and literal.

## Layout Principles

### The core rule

**No two consecutive slides should have the same layout.** Repeating the same structure (centred headline, body text, image right) creates visual monotony and makes the deck feel templated. Vary the grid, the text position, the image placement, and the balance of space across every slide.

### Slide structure

**Lead with navy.** The default slide background should be dark navy or a navy-dominant composition, not white. White backgrounds are acceptable for data-heavy slides, comparison tables, or moments where the content needs maximum readability, but they should be the exception, not the default.

**Asymmetry over symmetry.** Centred layouts are the most overused pattern in B2B SaaS. Prefer left-aligned text with images or graphics offset to one side. Use the grid to create tension and movement, not to create balance.

**One idea per slide.** If a slide is trying to communicate two separate concepts, split it into two slides. Density is not the same as substance. A single well-presented idea with breathing space is more persuasive than a crowded slide.

**Headlines do the work.** Every slide headline should communicate the slide's core point on its own. If the headline is a label ("Our Methodology," "The Team"), rewrite it as a statement ("Every tool is grounded in how behaviour actually changes," "Six years, fifty organisations, eleven hundred people").

### Content density

- Maximum three bullet points per slide, and only when a list is genuinely the best format.
- Favour short sentences over bullet lists. Prose on a slide reads as more considered and less like a pitch template.
- If data is present, let the data be the visual. Large numbers, clear charts, and minimal annotation. Do not surround data with explanatory paragraphs.

### Spacing and margins

- Maintain generous margins. Content should never touch the edge of the slide.
- Leave at least 15-20% of every slide as open space. This is not wasted space. It is what makes the brand feel grounded rather than crowded.

### What to avoid in layout

- **Do not use the centred-headline-plus-four-equal-columns layout.** This is the default SaaS pattern (visible on Hu.Be's current website). It makes any content look templated regardless of how good it is.
- **Do not use icon grids** (4 or 6 icons in a symmetric grid, each with a short label). This is the second most common SaaS layout pattern and adds no visual distinction.
- **Do not place G2/Gartner badges or award logos in a row.** If third-party validation is mentioned, integrate it naturally into a sentence or a footnote, not as a trust-badge wall.
- **Do not use a scrolling or tiled logo bar** for client organisations. If client logos appear, limit them to 4-6 maximum, placed subtly, and never as the headline or organising principle of a slide.
- **Do not use a "meet the team" grid of headshots** unless the audience has specifically asked about the team. In a deck context, credibility comes from the work and the results, not from faces.
